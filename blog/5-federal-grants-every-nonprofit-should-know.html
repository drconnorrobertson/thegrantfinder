5 Federal Grants Every Nonprofit Should Know About in 2026

Published on March 3, 2026 | By The Grant Finder

Federal grants represent some of the largest and most accessible funding opportunities available to nonprofit organizations. With billions of dollars distributed annually through federal programs, understanding which grants are most relevant to your organization can dramatically increase your chances of securing funding. In this comprehensive guide, we'll explore five federal grants that every nonprofit should know about in 2026, examining what they fund, how to apply, and why they matter to your organization.

1. Community Development Block Grants (CDBG)

The Community Development Block Grant program, administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), has been a cornerstone of community development funding for decades. CDBG funds support a wide range of community development activities, including affordable housing, economic development, public infrastructure, and social services for low- and moderate-income individuals.

What makes CDBG grants particularly valuable is their flexibility. Unlike many federal grants that restrict funding to specific activities, CDBG funds can be used for various purposes as long as they benefit low- and moderate-income communities. In 2026, CDBG programs distributed approximately $4 billion annually across thousands of cities and counties, making it one of the most widely available federal funding sources.

To apply for CDBG funding, your nonprofit typically needs to work with your local city or county government, as these jurisdictions control how CDBG funds are allocated within their areas. Building relationships with your local government officials and understanding their community development priorities is essential for accessing this funding stream.

2. Nonprofit Security Grant Initiative (NSGI)

The Department of Homeland Security's Nonprofit Security Grant Initiative provides funding to nonprofits to enhance their physical and cybersecurity measures. With increasing threats to nonprofit facilities and rising cybersecurity risks, NSGI grants have become increasingly critical for organizations across all sectors.

NSGI grants can fund a wide range of security improvements, including physical security enhancements like access control systems, surveillance equipment, and emergency communication systems. Additionally, nonprofits can use these grants to strengthen their cybersecurity infrastructure, implement protective software, and provide staff training on security best practices.

The grant program is highly competitive, with NSGI receiving tens of thousands of applications annually for funding. However, nonprofits that can articulate a clear security vulnerability and demonstrate how funding would address that vulnerability stand a strong chance of receiving awards. NSGI grants typically range from $10,000 to $100,000, making them accessible to organizations of varying sizes.

3. Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) Grants

SAMHSA, a division of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, distributes billions of dollars annually to support mental health and substance abuse prevention, treatment, and recovery services. Organizations working in behavioral health, including nonprofits, can access numerous grant programs through SAMHSA.

SAMHSA grant programs include funding for community mental health centers, substance abuse treatment facilities, peer recovery support services, and prevention programs. The breadth of SAMHSA funding is remarkable, with specific grants designed for different populations, service models, and geographic areas. Whether your nonprofit operates a residential treatment facility, provides outpatient counseling, or runs prevention education programs, SAMHSA likely has a grant program that could support your work.

One of the most accessible SAMHSA grant programs for smaller nonprofits is the Recovery Community Services Program, which provides funding for peer-led recovery support services. These grants recognize the power of lived experience and peer support in recovery, making them ideal for nonprofits built on peer-led models.

4. AmeriCorps Grants

AmeriCorps grants, administered by the Corporation for National and Community Service, support nonprofits in recruiting, training, and deploying service members who work on critical community challenges. These grants essentially provide your nonprofit with funding to hire service members who work full-time on mission-driven activities while receiving a modest stipend and education award.

AmeriCorps programs can significantly expand a nonprofit's capacity without requiring equivalent funding for permanent staff. Service members commit 1,700 hours of service over the course of a year, which translates to roughly ten full-time equivalent positions for many organizations. Additionally, AmeriCorps members often bring enthusiasm, energy, and diverse perspectives to nonprofits, creating a positive organizational culture.

AmeriCorps grants are highly competitive, but nonprofits with clear program goals, strong organizational infrastructure, and demonstrated ability to support and supervise service members have excellent chances of success. With approximately 75,000 AmeriCorps positions available nationally, this represents one of the largest federal workforce development programs.

5. Grants.gov Catalog Grants for Education and Social Services

While Grants.gov itself is not a grant source but rather a searchable federal grants database, certain consistent grant programs listed there are essential for nonprofits to know about. Education-related nonprofits should be aware of Department of Education grants for afterschool programs, youth mentoring, and literacy initiatives. Social service nonprofits should explore Department of Labor grants for workforce development and Department of Education grants for family engagement programs.

The specific grants available fluctuate based on federal priorities and appropriations, but consistently available programs include 21st Century Community Learning Centers, which provides funding for afterschool and summer programs for low-income students. These grants can range from $50,000 to several hundred thousand dollars annually, making them attractive to nonprofits serving youth and families.

Key Strategies for Federal Grant Success

Successfully securing federal grants requires more than simply knowing which grants exist. Nonprofits should develop several key strategies to maximize their chances of success. First, establish relationships with your federal grants officer or agency contact. These individuals can provide guidance on priorities, application requirements, and feedback on your proposal.

Second, allow adequate time for proposal development. Federal grants typically require extensive documentation, budget narratives, and evaluation plans. Most experts recommend allowing at least two to three months for proposal development. Many organizations that lose federal grant competitions lose because they rushed their applications in the final days before deadlines.

Third, ensure your organization's infrastructure is strong. Federal grants come with compliance requirements, reporting obligations, and audit requirements. Having solid financial management, clear policies, and experienced staff positions your organization favorably in the eyes of federal funders.

Conclusion

Federal grants represent tremendous opportunities for nonprofits, but accessing these opportunities requires knowledge, preparation, and persistence. The five federal grant programs we've discussed in this article represent just a small fraction of the federal funding available to nonprofits in 2026. By understanding these key programs and developing a strategic approach to federal grantmaking, your nonprofit can access resources to significantly expand your mission impact.

Start by assessing which of these five grant programs aligns most closely with your nonprofit's mission and current capacity. Contact the relevant federal agency, review past grant awards to understand what types of organizations succeed, and begin developing your proposal strategy. The federal grants landscape is vast, but with the right knowledge and approach, your nonprofit can access the federal funding it deserves.
